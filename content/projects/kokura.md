## Overview

The old town around Kokura Station is among the most densely built districts in East Asia, laid out at the end of the last century and still in demand for additional space. Large construction equipment cannot reach the site, so the project asks for a construction method that is easy to install, easy to change, and able to bring new life to the old city without disturbing what is already there.

Taking Le Corbusier's Modulor as its starting point, the scheme adopts a cubic module with a 3-metre modulus as the basic unit of space. A family of module types is developed for different uses, and the blocks are assembled on site according to where people actually move: a shopping mall where the station square is busiest, terraces and split levels that open into courtyards, and green facades shaped by planting.

Because of the long rainy season, the new buildings are connected to each other and to Kokura Station through interior space. The upper level joins the surrounding buildings so their functions can penetrate one another; the lower level, shaded by the floor above, covers the path of the crowd entering the station and shelters it from wind and rain.

## Focus

- Modular construction with a 3 m modulus
- Assembly and removal on a constrained urban site
- Function determined by pedestrian flow
- Station square and shopping programme
- Terraces, split levels, and courtyards
- Covered connection between station and district
- Climate-responsive public space

## Role

Academic and studio project, Kokura, Japan, September to October 2016. Team of three; I was the chief designer, responsible for concept development, the general structure, and modelling.
