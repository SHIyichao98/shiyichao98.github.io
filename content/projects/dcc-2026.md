## Overview

This research develops a benchmark-specific prototype for shape grammar inference from vector CAD drawings. Rather than starting from a predefined grammar, the workflow begins with geometric examples and attempts to recover a working vocabulary, local spatial relations, and executable rule schemata. The method is organized into five stages: Perception and Clustering, Lexical Labeling, Spatial Relations, Rule Deduction, and Generation and Validation.

The implementation operates on DXF drawings and uses DBSCAN to stabilize recurring polygonal units without predefining the number of shape types. Relation graphs then encode local geometric relations, and graph-based mining is used to derive candidate shape rules that can be executed and tested. The workflow is evaluated using Stiny's Two Exercises in Formal Composition, first on the published reference designs and then on additional designs regenerated from the reference rules.

## Focus

- Shape Grammar Inference
- Unsupervised Clustering with DBSCAN
- CAD Geometry Analysis
- Spatial Relation Graphs
- Graph-Based Rule Mining
- Generation-Based Validation

First author and lead researcher, responsible for the research framework, inference workflow, CAD geometry processing, clustering and relation analysis, rule-recovery methodology, benchmark evaluation, and preparation of the research publication.
